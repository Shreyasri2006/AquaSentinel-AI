SAFER DRINKING WATER

When water safety is uncertain

Follow directions from local authorities. If microbiological contamination is suspected and no safer source is available, boiling can kill germs. It does not remove harmful chemicals.

  1. 1
    Choose the safest source

    Prefer an approved bottled or protected water source when available.

  2. 2
    Clarify cloudy water

    Allow particles to settle or filter through a clean cloth before boiling.

  3. 3
    Bring water to a rolling boil

    CDC guidance recommends one minute, or three minutes above approximately 2,000 metres elevation.

  4. 4
    Cool and store safely

    Use a clean, sanitised container with a tight cover and avoid hand contact.

SEEK PROFESSIONAL CARE

Warning signs that should not be ignored

Contact a healthcare professional urgently when illness is severe, rapidly worsening or affecting a vulnerable person.

!
01

Unable to drink

Inability to drink or keep fluids down can quickly worsen dehydration.

02

Severe dehydration

Very little urine, unusual sleepiness, confusion, sunken eyes or extreme weakness requires attention.

03

Persistent vomiting

Repeated vomiting can prevent effective oral rehydration.

04

Blood in stool

Blood in stool, severe abdominal pain or high fever should be assessed professionally.

05

Rapid worsening

Sudden deterioration or very frequent watery diarrhoea may require immediate treatment.

06

Vulnerable person affected

Infants, young children, older adults, pregnant people and people with existing illness may need earlier assessment.

MYTHS AND FACTS

Correcting common water-safety misunderstandings

Myth

Clear-looking water is always safe to drink.

Fact

Water may look clear while still containing harmful microorganisms or chemicals.

Myth

Boiling removes every type of contamination.

Fact

Boiling kills many disease-causing organisms, but it does not remove harmful chemical contamination.

Myth

Mild diarrhoea never requires attention.

Fact

Fluid loss can become dangerous, especially for children, older adults and people already unwell.
